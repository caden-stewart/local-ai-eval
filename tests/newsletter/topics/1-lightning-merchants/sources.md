# Topic
Why small merchants are testing bitcoin payments over Lightning

# [S1] Cascade Merchant Survey 2026 (excerpt)
The Cascade Merchant Survey polled 1,150 independent retailers and restaurants in Oregon and Washington in spring 2026. 8% said they currently accept bitcoin over the Lightning Network, up from 3% in 2024. Among those that accept it, the most cited reason was lower processing costs (62%), followed by customer requests (24%). Among merchants that don't accept it, 47% said they didn't know how to start and 29% said they worried about price swings. Of accepting merchants, 71% convert some or all bitcoin receipts to dollars within a day. The survey did not measure what share of each merchant's sales came through Lightning.

# [S2] Interview notes: owner of Bramble Bike Works, Bend, Oregon
Tessa Morgan has accepted Lightning at her bike shop for 14 months. She estimates it accounts for about 5% of sales, mostly from repeat customers. "The fee savings are nice, but honestly the bigger thing is these customers come back," she said. Her biggest headache is accounting: her bookkeeper needed several hours to set up a process for recording bitcoin receipts at the dollar value on the day of sale. Refunds have been rare, but awkward when they happen. She keeps about a quarter of her Lightning receipts in bitcoin and converts the rest.

# [S3] Processor fee comparison (Tri-County Small Business Center fact sheet)
For a typical $40 purchase, the fact sheet compares costs. Card processing averaged $1.26, or about 3.15% including the per-transaction fee. A Lightning payment through a merchant payment app averaged $0.22, or 0.55%, including the app's conversion fee when the merchant converts to dollars immediately. If the merchant keeps the bitcoin rather than converting, the app fee drops to about $0.08, but the merchant then carries the price risk. The fact sheet notes that fees vary by provider and that it does not account for chargeback protection, which cards offer and Lightning does not.
