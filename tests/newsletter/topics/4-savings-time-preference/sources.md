# Topic
Why people stopped saving, and the small fix that worked

# [S1] Harbor County Credit Union member data brief
The average member savings rate fell from 11.2% of income in 2021 to 6.4% in 2025, after deposit rates were cut close to zero. Members under 30 hold median savings of $1,900. 38% of members under 30 carried a credit card balance for three or more consecutive months in 2025, up from 26% in 2021.

# [S2] Round-up program evaluation (Harbor County Credit Union, internal study)
The credit union launched an optional round-up program in 2024: debit card purchases round up to the next dollar and the difference moves to savings. 9,400 members enrolled in the first year. Enrolled members increased total savings by 14% more over twelve months than a matched group of similar members who didn't enroll. The study notes that members who chose to enroll may already have been more motivated to save, so part of the difference may not be caused by the program.

# [S3] Commentary by economist Marcus Bell
Bell reads the decline through the lens of time preference: interest is the price of waiting, and when that price falls to nearly nothing, people rationally shift toward consuming now. "People didn't become less disciplined overnight. The signal telling them to save got switched off." He recommends automatic transfers to savings on payday as the most reliable personal habit, because it removes the need to decide to save each time.
