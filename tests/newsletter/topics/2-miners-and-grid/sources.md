# Topic
Bitcoin miners as a shock absorber for the power grid

# [S1] Mesa Grid demand response summary, summer 2026
Mesa Grid enrolled 9 bitcoin mining sites, totaling 410 megawatts, in its flexible load program. Participants agree to power down within 10 minutes when the grid operator calls an event. During summer 2026 the utility called 37 curtailment events, totaling 212 hours. At the peak event on July 22, miners released 396 MW, which the utility said avoided firing an older gas peaker plant. Participating miners received credits worth an average of $2.1 million per site over the season.

# [S2] Op-ed by Dr. Walter Nguyen, grid reliability researcher
Nguyen argues that flexible miners are useful but not a substitute for new generation and transmission. "A load that can turn off is valuable during a heat wave, but it doesn't keep the lights on during a week-long cold snap when the shortage is supply, not a few peak hours." He also warns that if too much of the grid's reliability plan depends on miners, a crash in bitcoin mining profitability could remove that flexibility quickly, since unprofitable miners may shut down permanently rather than stand ready.

# [S3] Statement from the Southwest Ratepayers Coalition
The coalition supports demand response programs but questions whether credits paid to miners are priced fairly. It notes that residential customers who reduce use during events receive a flat $25 credit per season, while mining sites receive credits in the millions. The coalition has asked regulators to publish how credits are calculated and to open the same per-megawatt rates to large commercial customers other than miners.
