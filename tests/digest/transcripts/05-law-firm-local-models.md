The Agent Brief, episode 44: A law firm that keeps its AI in-house

HOST (Sam Whitaker): My guest is Daniel Brennan, managing partner at Brennan & Oyelaran, a 40-person litigation firm. Daniel, you run AI models on your own hardware. Why not just use the cloud?

DANIEL: Client confidentiality. Our engagement letters promise that client documents don't leave our control. When we read the terms of the big cloud AI services, our malpractice carrier had questions we couldn't answer. So our rule is simple: no client data goes to a cloud AI service. Full stop.

SAM: What did you build instead?

DANIEL: We run open-weight models on two Mac Studios in a locked room in our office. Our IT contractor set it up. Associates reach it through the firm's internal network, and nothing is exposed to the internet.

SAM: What did it cost?

DANIEL: Setup was $14,800, including the hardware and the contractor's time. There's no per-use charge after that, which our partners like, because nobody has to ration it.

SAM: What do people use it for?

DANIEL: Mostly first-pass document review. An associate points it at a production of a few thousand documents and asks it to flag anything about a specific topic. Our measurement over the first quarter showed document review time down 35% on the matters where we used it.

SAM: What's gone wrong?

DANIEL: Citations. When we asked it to draft sections of briefs, it invented case citations that looked real. In the first 90 days our associates caught 17 fabricated citations before anything was filed. None reached a court, but it scared us.

SAM: What did you change?

DANIEL: Every brief that touched the model now requires a citation verification step. A paralegal checks every citation against our legal research database before a partner signs. We also stopped using it for drafting legal argument at all. It reviews; people write.

SAM: Would you do it again?

DANIEL: Yes. The review savings alone paid for the hardware within the year. But I'd tell any firm to treat it like a very fast first-year associate who sometimes makes things up. You check its work.

SAM: Daniel Brennan, thank you.
