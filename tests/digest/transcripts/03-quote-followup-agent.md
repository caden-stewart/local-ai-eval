The Agent Brief, episode 41: An AI agent that chases quotes

HOST (Sam Whitaker): My guest runs operations at Tidewater Supply, a plumbing distributor with about 60 employees. Rachel Kim, welcome.

RACHEL: Thanks, Sam.

SAM: You put an AI agent in charge of following up on quotes. Why that job?

RACHEL: Because it was the job nobody did well. Our inside sales team would send a quote to a contractor and then get pulled into the next phone call. By the time anyone followed up, the contractor had already bought from someone else.

SAM: How slow was it?

RACHEL: We measured it before the pilot. The average time from sending a quote to the first follow-up was 26 hours. With the agent, it's down to 3 hours. The agent drafts a follow-up based on the quote, the customer's history and what they bought last time, and it goes out the same morning.

SAM: Did it actually win more business?

RACHEL: Our quote win rate went from 31% to 37% over the six-month pilot. I don't credit all of that to the agent, since it was also a strong season, but the accounts where the agent was active improved more than the ones where it wasn't.

SAM: What does it cost?

RACHEL: About $1,150 a month, all in, for the software and the model usage. Compared with what one extra point of win rate is worth to us, that's small.

SAM: Tell me what went wrong. Something always goes wrong.

RACHEL: In the first month, the agent sent follow-ups with the wrong pricing on 14 quotes. It had pulled an outdated price list. Contractors noticed, and two of them called to complain.

SAM: How did you fix it?

RACHEL: We added a human approval step. The agent drafts every follow-up, but a salesperson has to approve anything that mentions a price before it goes out. It takes them a few seconds per message, and we haven't had a pricing error since.

SAM: Did anyone lose their job?

RACHEL: No. Two people who used to spend most of their day on follow-ups were reassigned to account management, which is what they wanted to be doing anyway.

SAM: Rachel Kim, thank you.
