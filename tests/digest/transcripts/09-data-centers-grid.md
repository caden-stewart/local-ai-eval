The Agent Brief, episode 50: AI data centers and the power grid

HOST (Sam Whitaker): My guest is Carmen Reyes, director of planning at Mesa Grid, a utility in the Southwest. Carmen, how much new demand are you seeing from AI data centers?

CARMEN: Data center developers have requested 3.2 gigawatts of new connections in our territory. For context, our entire system peak today is 5.9 gigawatts. If all of it got built, it would be like adding half again our current peak.

SAM: Will it all get built?

CARMEN: No. A lot of those requests are speculative. Developers file in several places and build in one. But even a fraction of it is more new load than we've added in decades.

SAM: When does the first one come online?

CARMEN: The first large data center connection is scheduled for 2029. It takes years to build the transmission and generation to serve something that size.

SAM: Who pays for all that?

CARMEN: That's the fight. We've created a new large-load tariff. Any customer asking for this much power has to sign a 15-year contract, so if they leave early, regular customers aren't stuck paying for infrastructure built for them.

SAM: Will household bills go up anyway?

CARMEN: We've asked regulators for a 4.7% rate increase, mostly for grid upgrades that serve everyone. We're trying hard to keep data center costs from flowing into that.

SAM: You also have bitcoin miners on your system. How do they fit?

CARMEN: They've turned out to be useful. Our mining customers agreed to shut down when the grid is stressed. Last summer we curtailed them for 212 hours during heat waves, which freed up capacity for air conditioning when it mattered most. We're asking whether some data center workloads can be flexible in the same way.

SAM: Can they?

CARMEN: Training runs maybe. Serving live AI requests, much less so. That's what we're studying now.

SAM: Carmen Reyes, thank you.
