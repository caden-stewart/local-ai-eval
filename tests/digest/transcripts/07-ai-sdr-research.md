The Agent Brief, episode 47: Research agents for sales teams

HOST (Sam Whitaker): I'm talking with Nina Castellanos, VP of sales at Crestline Robotics, which sells warehouse automation. Nina, you gave your sales development team an AI research agent. What does it do?

NINA: Before a rep reaches out to an account, the agent reads the company's recent news, job postings and earnings materials, and writes a short brief plus a suggested first line for the email. We ran it across 600 target accounts last quarter.

SAM: Did it help?

NINA: Meetings booked went up 28% compared with the quarter before, with the same team size. Our email reply rate went from 2.6% to 4.1%.

SAM: What did reps think?

NINA: They were skeptical at first, then they loved getting their mornings back. Research used to take them close to two hours a day. Now it's reading briefs over coffee.

SAM: What went wrong?

NINA: Personalization mistakes. In the first month, 13% of the suggested first lines referenced the wrong company news: an acquisition that belonged to a different company with a similar name, or a press release from three years ago presented as new. A prospect who gets "congrats on the acquisition" when there wasn't one doesn't reply.

SAM: How did you fix that?

NINA: Two changes. We made the agent cite the source link for every claim in its brief, and we made reps review the first line of every email before sending. That review takes seconds when the source link is right there. Since then, the error rate has been close to zero.

SAM: Would you let the agent send emails on its own?

NINA: Not yet. The research is where it saves time. The send is where a mistake costs you the account, so a human stays on that step.

SAM: Nina Castellanos, thank you.
