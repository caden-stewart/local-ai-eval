Sound Money Hour, episode 212: Miners after the halving

HOST (Luis Ferreira): Welcome back. My guest today is Priya Natarajan, head of research at Ridgeline Hash Research. Priya, you just published your post-halving miner survey. What did you actually do?

PRIYA: We surveyed 47 mining firms, public and private, between May and August. We asked for their power costs, their machine fleets, and what they planned to do over the next year and a half. Most of them answered in more detail than I expected, probably because everyone is nervous.

LUIS: Nervous about what, specifically?

PRIYA: Revenue per unit of hashrate. The industry calls it hashprice. Before the halving it was sitting around $61 per petahash per day. By August it had fallen to $38. That's the number that keeps finance teams up at night, because their costs didn't fall with it.

LUIS: So what does it cost to mine a bitcoin now?

PRIYA: The median all-in cost across our respondents came in at $71,400 per bitcoin. That includes power, hosting, staff and depreciation on the machines. Some firms are well below that, some are far above it.

LUIS: What separates the ones below it?

PRIYA: Power, almost entirely. The only firms that told us they were still clearly profitable had power contracts under 4.5 cents per kilowatt-hour. Newer machines help at the margin, but a great machine on expensive power loses to a mediocre machine on cheap power.

LUIS: I hear a lot of talk about miners moving to other countries.

PRIYA: Some are. We asked directly, and based on their answers we expect roughly 12% of network hashrate to either go offline or relocate within 18 months. Relocation is mostly toward places with stranded energy, like flared gas sites or regions with more hydro than the grid can use.

LUIS: Is that bad for the network?

PRIYA: Not in the long run. Difficulty adjusts, and the remaining miners become more efficient. It's painful for the firms involved, but the protocol was designed for exactly this.

LUIS: Last question. What's the one thing an individual listener should take from this?

PRIYA: That mining is a business with very thin margins. If someone pitches you a mining investment, the first question is what they pay for power, and the second is what happens to them if hashprice drops another 20%.

LUIS: Priya Natarajan, thank you.
