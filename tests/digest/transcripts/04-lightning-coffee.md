Sound Money Hour, episode 215: Paying for coffee over Lightning

HOST (Luis Ferreira): I'm joined by Omar Haddad, who owns Northfork Coffee. Omar, how big is Northfork now?

OMAR: We have 23 locations across two states. We started accepting bitcoin over the Lightning Network about a year ago.

LUIS: Why would a coffee chain bother?

OMAR: Honestly, card fees. Card processing costs us about 2.9% of every sale once you add everything up. On Lightning, our all-in cost has been around 0.4%. On coffee margins, that difference is real money.

LUIS: How many customers actually use it?

OMAR: Lightning is 2.8% of our transactions right now. That sounds small, but it was close to zero when we started, and it's been growing every quarter. The customers who use it tend to be regulars, and they come in more often.

LUIS: What about the staff? Was it hard to train them?

OMAR: Easier than I feared. The point-of-sale terminal shows a QR code, the customer scans it, done. Most baristas learned it in one shift.

LUIS: What's been the hardest part?

OMAR: Refunds, by far. With a card, a refund is a button. With Lightning, the customer has to give us an invoice to send the money back to, and half of them don't know how. We've had people wait at the counter for five minutes while we figure it out. That's the single biggest complaint from our managers.

LUIS: Have you solved it?

OMAR: Partly. We now offer store credit as the default for Lightning refunds, and people mostly accept that. But I want the wallet makers to make refunds as easy as paying.

LUIS: What's next for Northfork?

OMAR: We're rolling Lightning out to 11 more stores we're opening next year, so every new location will take it from day one.

LUIS: Would you recommend it to other small businesses?

OMAR: If your margins are thin and your customers ask for it, yes. Just plan for the refunds before you launch, not after.

LUIS: Omar Haddad, thanks for coming on.
