Sound Money Hour, episode 218: How people actually hold their bitcoin

HOST (Luis Ferreira): Today I have Grace Okafor, who led the Keystone Custody Survey. Grace, what was the survey?

GRACE: We asked 2,400 bitcoin holders in the United States how they store their coins, whether they've ever lost access, and what went wrong if they did.

LUIS: Start with the headline.

GRACE: 41% of respondents keep all of their bitcoin on an exchange. They don't hold their own keys at all. Among people who started buying in the last two years, that share is even higher.

LUIS: That's a lot of trust in exchanges.

GRACE: It is, and many of them told us they know the risk. They just find self-custody intimidating and worry they'll lose everything through their own mistake.

LUIS: Is that worry justified?

GRACE: Partly. 16% of respondents who had ever used their own wallet said they had lost access to a wallet at some point. Some got access back; many didn't.

LUIS: What's the most common way people lose access?

GRACE: By far, it's how they stored the seed phrase. People take a screenshot of it, or email it to themselves, or keep it in a notes app. Then a phone dies, an account gets hacked, or a cloud backup gets deleted. Storing the seed phrase digitally was the single most common failure we found.

LUIS: So what should people do?

GRACE: For anyone holding an amount that would really hurt to lose, our recommendation is a 2-of-3 multisig setup. You have three keys stored in different places, and any two of them can move the funds. Losing one key isn't a disaster, and a thief who finds one key can't do anything with it.

LUIS: Isn't multisig complicated?

GRACE: It used to be. The tools have improved a lot. It's still more work than leaving coins on an exchange, but it's the setup that best matches how people actually make mistakes.

LUIS: Grace Okafor, thank you.
